Four Possible Diagnostic Outcomes
Every diagnostic decision falls into one of four categories:

Patient has the disease.
Test correctly returns positive.
False Negative (FN)

Test incorrectly returns negative.
Important risk: missed diagnosis.
True Negative (TN)
Patient does not have the disease.
Test correctly returns negative.
False Positive (FP)
Patient does not have the disease.
Test incorrectly returns positive.
May lead to unnecessary follow-up tests.
Clinical Perspective

Ethics and Assumptions
Statistical conclusions depend on assumptions.
Convergence results may require finite variance.
Heavy-tailed data can behave very differently from Gaussian data.
Removing outliers simply to “force Normality” can introduce bias.
Critical Question
Is an unusual observation:
a genuine anomaly or a legitimate rare event?
Good statistical practice
Understand the data-generating process.
Check assumptions rather than blindly enforcing them.
Report uncertainty honestly.
